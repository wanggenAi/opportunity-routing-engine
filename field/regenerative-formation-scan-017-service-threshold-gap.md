## 2. Demotion — exact-spec residual repair materials

### Observed pressure

Localized repair of tile/flooring/stone can require an exact model, size, color number or production batch. A few leftover pieces can therefore have low commodity value but very high avoided-rework value.

This is a real asymmetry:

```text
LOW RESIDUAL MATERIAL PRICE
+
HIGH AVOIDED REPLACEMENT COST
```

### Why it is not retained

The identity key is already becoming normal market metadata.

Current evidence includes:
- China tile channels explicitly selling development/project-specific replacement tile such as "碧桂园精装房同款" and repair/replenishment tile;
- a current tile-finding app advertising image search, rare/discontinued-tile purchase requests, same-city stock and idle-tile resale;
- established B2B "找砖/调货" workflows;
- international surplus-building-material markets that already list specific-batch tile/flooring and nearby project leftovers.

Verdict:

**EXACT-SPEC RESIDUAL MATERIAL = DEMOTED / NORMALIZING SPECIALTY MARKET.**

Do not rediscover it as a generic leftover-material marketplace.

**GENERIC CROSS-BORDER PHYSICAL PROOF = DEMOTED / CONTROL PLANE ALREADY OWNED.**

## 4. Retained formation — sparse installed-base local service threshold

Working name:

**SPARSE INSTALLED-BASE SERVICE THRESHOLD GAP**

Alternative shorthand:

**MID-BASE AFTER-SALES GAP**

### 4.1 Surface structure

A Chinese or other exporting industrial-equipment OEM may have:
- real installed equipment in an ASEAN country;
- recurring customer expectations for installation, commissioning, troubleshooting, maintenance and spare-parts support;
- too few machines in-country to justify an owned service center, dedicated local engineer and stocked warehouse;
- too much service obligation to rely indefinitely on remote video calls or flying an engineer from the home country.

The hypothesized threshold is:

```text
ZERO / TINY INSTALLED BASE
→ REMOTE SUPPORT IS TOLERABLE

SPARSE BUT GROWING INSTALLED BASE
→ LOCAL RESPONSE BECOMES NECESSARY
→ OWNED LOCAL SERVICE IS STILL UNECONOMIC

DENSE INSTALLED BASE
→ OEM / DISTRIBUTOR CAN INTERNALIZE SERVICE
```

The middle state is the retained formation.

## 8. Working latent connection

Do **not** promote this yet.

The working connection is:

```text
OEM WITH SPARSE INSTALLED BASE
+ PRODUCT KNOWLEDGE / REMOTE EXPERT / PARTS
↕
UNRESOLVED TRAINING + TRUST + DISPATCH + WARRANTY BOUNDARY
↕
LOCAL MULTI-BRAND INDUSTRIAL SERVICE CAPACITY
+ GEOGRAPHIC PROXIMITY + GENERAL TECHNICAL SKILL
→
END CUSTOMER REQUIRING FAST RESTORE / COMMISSIONING / MAINTENANCE
```

Potential routing keys:
- country / city / industrial zone;
- equipment class;
- electrical/mechanical/controls capability;
- required certification;
- model-specific training state;
- tooling;
- travel radius;
- SLA availability;
- language;
- warranty authorization;
- spare-parts access.

This is materially different from a consumer repair marketplace because the OEM may remain the technical authority while local capacity performs bounded field work.

## 9. Why retained

The formation survives the ATTRIBUTE-COLLAPSE FILTER for now because:
1. service demand begins after a physical installed base exists, not merely as listing metadata;
2. the OEM and local technician/service firm are genuinely separate nodes;
3. repeated partner recruitment and strategic service partnerships show connection pressure;
4. sparse installed base plausibly makes both pure remote support and full local internalization inefficient;
5. local multi-brand capability exists in at least some equipment families;
6. the orchestration problem includes certification, authorization, documentation, parts and SLA — recurring value beyond introduction.

But these points are not enough for commercial promotion.

## 10. Decisive unknowns

The formation must be killed unless evidence resolves:

1. **Installed-base threshold**
   - At what installed-base/service-call density does an OEM actually switch from fly-in engineers to a third-party/local partner?
   - Is the middle zone long-lived enough to be a market, or only a short transitional phase?

2. **Payer**
   - Does the OEM pay per case, retainer or warranty reserve?
   - Does the distributor own service economics?
   - Does the end customer pay only out of warranty?

3. **Capability portability**
   - Which equipment families permit a local multi-brand technician to execute bounded work after OEM remote diagnosis/training?
   - Which are too proprietary, safety-critical or software-locked?

4. **Authorization / liability**
   - Who accepts responsibility for warranty work, safety, site access, damage and failed repair?
   - Can the OEM authorize a third party without destroying warranty/customer trust?

5. **Learning cost**
   - How much model-specific training/documentation/tooling is required before a technician is useful?
   - Does learning cost exceed expected case flow for sparse installed bases?

6. **Parts boundary**
   - Can the local node diagnose and swap modules while the OEM supplies parts?
   - Or does parts inventory/local customs dominate downtime so strongly that technician routing is secondary?

7. **Control-plane ownership**
   - Is the distributor already the natural owner in most commercially viable cases?
   - Are independent service partners willing to work white-label for multiple OEMs?

8. **Normalized economics**
   - OEM service fee / retainer
   - minus local technician payout
   - travel
   - training/certification
   - QA/rework/liability
   - translation/coordination
   - parts handling
   = normalized orchestration margin.
