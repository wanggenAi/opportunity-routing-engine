## 1. Question

Scan 017 established a plausible service-density threshold:

```text
REMOTE / FLY-IN SUPPORT
→ SPARSE BUT GROWING INSTALLED BASE
→ LOCAL RESPONSE BECOMES NECESSARY
→ FULL OWNED LOCAL SERVICE NETWORK BECOMES ECONOMIC
```

Scan 018 asks the decisive question:

> Does the middle zone create an **unowned orchestration edge**, or is an authorized local-service-partner network already the normal institutional solution?

## 2. Threshold mechanism survives

The threshold itself is real enough to retain as a market-structure primitive.

A 2026 Midea Southeast Asia interview describes three service-network stages:
- ordinary authorized service outlets;
- exclusive outlets when the manufacturer can provide sufficient stable order volume;
- self-built outlets in strategic markets.

Source:
https://www.21jingji.com/article/20260907/herald/056de761fa8b56aa2c6519b462690764.html

This is consumer appliances, not industrial equipment, so it is **mechanism evidence only**. It supports the general proposition that service density can change the efficient organizational form.

Industrial-market guides likewise describe local-partner, regional-network and direct/owned support models changing with installed-base and service intensity. Exact unit counts in such guides are not treated as hard thresholds.

## 3. But the middle zone already has a normal institutional form

### 3.1 OEM-authorized local service partner

The observed solution is not unusual or improvised.

Examples:
- Nikkiso formed a 2026 strategic service partnership with Exion Asia to use an established Southeast Asian workshop and field-service network.
- Alfa Laval operates channel/service partner programs.
- local firms in Thailand, Malaysia and Indonesia publicly provide installation, commissioning, repair and maintenance, including OEM-authorized or certified relationships.
- Chinese robot makers recruit overseas service-provider partners and provide training/support.

This means:

```text
SPARSE INSTALLED BASE
→ PARTNER / DISTRIBUTOR / AUTHORIZED SERVICE NODE
```

is already a recognized institutional response.

### 3.2 White-label OEM field service is already a product category

Global providers already sell essentially the hypothesized orchestration layer.

Noch offers OEMs a white-label field network with:
- technicians trained to OEM procedures;
- warranty support;
- commissioning;
- corrective and preventive maintenance;
- documentation and escalation.

Source:
https://nochpower.com/partners

Quest International likewise sells outcome-based, white-label field execution for OEMs, including installation, integration, maintenance, upgrades and training.

Source:
https://www.questinc.com/oemservices/field-services

Prohoc describes global field service as mobilizing the right expertise for OEM assembly, installation, commissioning, maintenance, repair and upgrades.

Source:
https://prohoc.fi/the-invisible-backbone-of-global-industrial-service/

The core architecture therefore already exists:

```text
OEM TECHNICAL AUTHORITY
+
EXTERNAL FIELD CAPACITY
+
TRAINING / DISPATCH / SLA / DOCUMENTATION
```

It is not an undiscovered business primitive.

It does **not** prove every ASEAN country and equipment category is efficiently covered. It does prove that language, local presence, commissioning, training and parts coordination can be integrated by an existing intermediary rather than requiring a new generic control plane.

## 5. Local field capacity is not the scarce universal resource

Thailand, Malaysia and Indonesia already contain independent industrial service firms with installation, maintenance, commissioning and multi-brand capability.

Examples include:
- TMA Thailand: installation, commissioning, relocation, maintenance and multilingual local support;
- FERSMEK Thailand: local service capability with OEM certification in selected equipment;
- Ojala Indonesia: multi-brand industrial robot service and an official KUKA service relationship;
- Malaysian engineering/service companies providing installation, maintenance and commissioning.

Therefore:

```text
LOCAL TECHNICIAN EXISTS
```

is not the decisive unresolved truth.

The harder constraints are normally:
- OEM authorization;
- model-specific knowledge;
- proprietary software/tools;
- warranty allocation;
- parts access;
- documentation;
- liability;
- enough case density to justify training.

Those constraints are controlled primarily by the OEM, distributor or an already-contracted service partner.

## 6. Parts and supportability often sit upstream of dispatch

Current China-to-Thailand machinery service material explicitly treats supportability as a pre-purchase design problem:
- wear-parts BOM;
- warranty start;
- engineer legal work days;
- spare-parts customs;
- local availability of standard components;
- OEM dependence for proprietary parts.

Source:
https://www.aseanmachine.com/en/china-sourcing

The implication is important:

```text
FAST LOCAL HANDS
WITHOUT
PARTS + DOCUMENTATION + AUTHORIZATION
!=
FAST RESTORATION
```

Technician routing alone cannot own the outcome.

## 7. Competing structural hypotheses

### H1 — Local technician discovery is the missing edge

**REJECTED AS PRIMARY.**

Local multi-brand service firms and field networks already exist.

### H2 — OEM-to-local training/certification is the missing edge

**NOT AN OPEN GENERIC EDGE.**

Training/certification is already a standard component of authorized partner models and white-label service programs.

### H3 — Cross-border language/contract/payment is the missing edge

**WEAKENED / NOT PROVEN DECISIVE.**

Existing China–Thailand operators already integrate bilingual teams, local entities, local engineers, acceptance, after-sales and parts commitments.

### H4 — Sparse service density creates a real organizational threshold

**SUPPORTED.**

But a real threshold is not itself an unowned control plane.

### H5 — The economic solution is a partner/distributor/service network selected by vertical and geography

**SUPPORTED / GOVERNING FORM.**

The middle zone appears to be served through a family of existing institutional forms rather than a universal new marketplace.

## 8. Causal frontier

The causal chain resolves to:

```text
FOREIGN EQUIPMENT SALE
→ PHYSICAL INSTALLED BASE
→ CUSTOMER UPTIME / COMMISSIONING OBLIGATION
→ LOCAL RESPONSE PRESSURE RISES WITH INSTALLED BASE
→ OWNED SERVICE NETWORK REMAINS TOO EXPENSIVE AT LOW DENSITY
→ OEM USES DISTRIBUTOR / AUTHORIZED PARTNER / WHITE-LABEL FIELD SERVICE
→ MODEL-SPECIFIC TRAINING + WARRANTY + PARTS + DOCUMENTATION REMAIN OEM-GOVERNED
```

Causal stop reason:

**INTERVENTION_RELEVANT_BOUNDARY**

The threshold is real, but the normal intervention boundary is already an authorized service relationship.

## 9. Missing-edge verdict

No independent generic missing edge is established.

To create a new commercial layer, an operator would need a privileged wedge such as:
- one equipment family with highly portable bounded procedures;
- one geography with demonstrably missing authorized coverage;
- contracted OEM demand before building supply;
- proprietary training/diagnostic access;
- a parts/logistics advantage;
- or an existing installed-base relationship.

Without such a wedge, "aggregate technicians for Chinese OEMs in ASEAN" reproduces an existing service-partner / field-service business and requires founder-heavy partner acquisition and trust building.
