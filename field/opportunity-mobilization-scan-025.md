# The evidence boundary

The repeated active hiring remains meaningful.

It tells us:

> mature platforms have **not eliminated the internal responsibility function**.

But it does **not** yet tell us:

- whether these firms use the platforms;
- whether they tried them and rejected them;
- which responsibility cannot be externalized;
- whether the blocker is trust, authority, ERP access, technical judgment, confidentiality, supplier relationship, physical acceptance or something else;
- whether a third-party router can economically replace even part of the internal role.

Those explanations cannot be inferred.

Therefore:

# `PLATFORM COVERAGE + INTERNAL HIRING = RESIDUAL QUESTION, NOT RESIDUAL PROOF`

and:

# `ACTIVE HIRING != WILLINGNESS TO OUTSOURCE`

# Main learning

Scan 025 provides the strongest evidence so far that a recurring manufacturing control-plane function is economically real in Xuzhou.

Companies are actively paying humans for:

- supplier discovery;
- backup-source creation;
- capacity routing;
- external-production progress;
- quality acceptance;
- exception closure;
- supplier performance;
- export coordination.

But the next mistake would be to say:

> “therefore build a procurement outsourcing platform.”

That would reverse the doctrine.

The correct rule is:

# `PAID INTERNAL COORDINATOR != EXTERNAL ROUTING WHITESPACE`

The missing fact is not whether the work exists.

It is whether some part of that responsibility is already **externalizable and repeatedly paid outside the firm**, while the current provider route still leaves an observed residual.

Fresh Scan 026 keeps cost-first discipline but moves closer to commercial truth:

# `CURRENT EXTERNALIZED WORKAROUND FIRST`

Then require a directly observed residual such as:

- dissatisfaction;
- fallback to manual work;
- repeated switching;
- multi-provider fragmentation;
- missed delivery;

Do not retain a category merely because outsourcing exists.
