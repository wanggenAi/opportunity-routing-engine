## 2. Demotion — surplus MRO / obsolete spare inventory

### Why it looked attractive

Factories accumulate slow-moving and obsolete MRO inventory after:
- equipment retirement;
- project overbuy;
- line modification;
- model changes;
- preventive spare stocking.

Another factory can simultaneously face a line-down event for the same exact part.

That creates a powerful value asymmetry:

```text
SELLER:
DEAD / WRITTEN-DOWN INVENTORY

BUYER:
UPTIME-SAVING CRITICAL SPARE
```

### Why it is demoted

The control plane is already a developed market category.

Current examples include:
- Indess: verified plant/supplier catalogues for surplus and obsolete spares;
- SPARETECH: enterprise surplus identification and resale;
- MRO Exchange and specialist obsolete-component suppliers;
- reverse-engineering and on-demand machining when the exact spare is unavailable.

Sources:
- https://indess.app/parts
- https://indess.app/faq
- https://sparetech.io/solving-industrial-surplus
- https://mroexchange.com/blogs/the-mro-exchange-blog/industrial-surplus-market-trends-2026

Verdict:

**SURPLUS MRO ROUTING = DEMOTED / MATURE SPECIALTY MARKET.**

The exact-part-number identity key is already standardized.

## 5. Retained formation — legacy technical lineage

Working name:

**LEGACY TECHNICAL LINEAGE ROUTING**

Alternative shorthand:

**WHO ACTUALLY KNOWS THIS OLD MACHINE?**

### 5.1 Observed stranded value

A legacy production asset may still be economically useful after:
- the OEM ends support;
- the original controls become obsolete;
- documentation is incomplete;
- source code is lost;
- the engineer who designed or repeatedly patched the system retires or leaves;
- the current maintenance team only understands fragments.

The scarce resource is not generic engineering labor.

It is:

**MODEL / SYSTEM / PROJECT-SPECIFIC TACIT KNOWLEDGE HELD BY A PERSON WHO IS NO LONGER NATURALLY CONNECTED TO THE ASSET OWNER.**

Potential knowledge holders:
- former OEM field engineers;
- retired controls engineers;
- former machine builders;
- commissioning engineers;
- original system integrators;
- plant engineers who maintained the same equipment family;
- specialists from a predecessor company after acquisition or closure.

## 10. Strong counterevidence

The formation must not be confused with a generic "retired engineers marketplace."

### Existing legacy-service firms

Specialized integrators already take responsibility for:
- obsolete PLC/DCS migration;
- reverse engineering;
- logic recovery;
- retrofit;
- unsupported machinery service.

Examples:
- https://atgcontrols.com/system-migration-and-revamp/
- https://www.jcindustries.fr/en/maintenance-of-legacy-equipment/
- https://www.rgttechnology.com/

### Existing expert networks

General expert networks and specialist consulting firms already assemble retired/former executives and engineering specialists for cases.

Example:
https://newtroubleshooter.com/expert-network

### Reverse engineering can substitute for memory

Many mechanical/controls problems can be solved by:
- recovering logic;
- scanning parts;
- reverse engineering;
- retrofit;
- modern replacement.

Therefore historical human lineage is only valuable when it reduces uncertainty or recovery time beyond these substitutes.

## 11. Working structural hypothesis

The possible missing edge is not:

```text
FACTORY NEEDS ENGINEER
↔ ENGINEER WANTS WORK
```

That is ordinary consulting.

The more specific hypothesis is:

```text
LEGACY ASSET
+ MODEL / SERIAL / CONTROLS / PROJECT HISTORY
+ SUPPORT DISCONTINUITY
+ HIGH UNCERTAINTY / DOWNTIME COST
↕
TECHNICAL-LINEAGE GRAPH
↕
FORMER OEM / COMMISSIONING / MAINTENANCE PERSON
WITH DIRECT OR NEAR-DIRECT HISTORICAL KNOWLEDGE
```

Possible routing keys:
- OEM / predecessor company;
- machine family;
- model;
- serial range;
- PLC/DCS platform;
- year / project;
- commissioning site;
- former role;
- geography;
- industry;
- language;
- availability;
- conflict / NDA status.

The routing value would come from identifying **who is unusually likely to know the hidden history**, not merely who possesses a generic skill.

## 12. Why it survives the PRE-MARKET STRANDED VALUE filter

For now, it survives because:

1. **The resource exists before a market listing**
   - tacit historical knowledge sits in people, often without an active consulting listing.

2. **The value can be state-dependent and asymmetric**
   - the same retired engineer's memory may have little market value most days but high value during a line-down or migration.

3. **The resource is not naturally held by the current asset owner**
   - the person may have left years earlier.

4. **Manual referral pressure is observed**
   - forum/community search for retired model-specific expertise exists.

5. **Historical lineage may materially reduce search/recovery time**
   - the Motion case provides an executed example.

6. **The routing key can change the feasible counterparty set**
   - model/project lineage can identify a different person than a generic job-title search.

This is sufficient for causal descent, not for commercial promotion.

## 13. Decisive unknowns

The formation must be killed unless the next scan resolves:

1. **Necessity frequency**
   - How often is former-OEM/project-specific human knowledge actually decisive rather than merely convenient?

2. **Recoverability**
   - Can the right lineage be inferred from public employment, patents, project records, service history or professional communities without prohibited/private data?

3. **Legality / IP / confidentiality**
   - What knowledge can a former employee legally share?
   - How are trade secrets, source code, customer data and old employer obligations excluded?

4. **Safety / liability**
   - Can the engagement be limited to historical explanation, diagnostic context and advisory support rather than unauthorized safety-critical repair?

5. **Payer**
   - asset owner;
   - integrator;
   - insurer;
   - maintenance contractor;
   - legal/forensic team?

6. **Urgency economics**
   - Does avoiding days/weeks of downtime justify expert discovery, vetting and coordination fees?

7. **Supply willingness**
   - Will retired/former engineers accept short, bounded, on-demand consultations?

8. **Incumbent absorption**
   - Do legacy-service firms already maintain enough alumni networks that a neutral routing layer adds little?

9. **Founder dependence**
   - Can expert discovery/vetting become a repeatable data process, or does every case become boutique headhunting?
