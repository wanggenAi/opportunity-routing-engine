## 3. Retained formation zone: intergenerational housing × companionship

This is the only zone that survives the wide scan.

The current observed pattern is:

```text
YOUTH HOUSING PRESSURE
+
SUITABLE ELDERCARE / RESIDENTIAL CAPACITY
+
OLDER-ADULT NEED FOR ORDINARY HUMAN CONTACT / SOCIAL VITALITY
```

This is **not yet** the system's explanation of the structure.

- imbalanced eldercare-institution utilization;
- youth housing cost / commuting pressure;
- older residents' non-professional companionship needs;
- housing discounts exchanged for volunteer service;
- risk and policy standards;
- a possible municipal intergenerational co-living digital platform;

Source:

This is important because the pairing is not invented by this repository.

But it is also immediate counterevidence:

> Shanghai is already considering institutional/platform normalization.

Therefore:

`REAL FLOW = YES`

does **not** imply:

`OPEN PRIVATE ROUTING GAP = YES`

## 4. Why this formation is retained — but not promoted

It passes the minimum **research-retention** screen:

1. both sides are replenishing populations;
2. the pressure appears without founder selling;
3. exchange is already occurring in multiple places;
4. the exchange is more than generic matching;
5. value is multi-dimensional: space, time, companionship and belonging;
6. the same broad structure has survived across different institutions/cities.

But it fails the **promotion** screen because the causal structure is still ambiguous.

Possible youth latent outcomes include:
- lower/stabler housing cost;
- urban belonging;
- a low-intensity way to contribute without becoming a care worker.

Possible older-adult / institution outcomes include:
- ordinary companionship and social vitality;
- utilization of suitable underused space;
- participation of younger people in institutional community life.

These are hypotheses.

They are not yet selected outcomes.

## 5. Competing structural explanations

The next stage must discriminate among at least these explanations.

### H1 — Housing-price disequilibrium

The main engine is simply:
- youth cannot afford preferred housing;
- institutions can offer unusually cheap rooms.

If this is the dominant cause, ordinary subsidized/guaranteed rental housing may absorb the pressure.

### H2 — Underused institutional residential capacity

The main engine is:
- eldercare operators hold suitable but underused residential capacity;
- the marginal value of youth occupancy is positive.

If true, the key evidence must be:
- persistent suitable spare capacity;
- permission to use it;
- economics after supervision/management cost.

### H3 — Older-adult social-contact deficit

The main engine is:
- professional care does not generate enough ordinary intergenerational contact;
- young residents produce a kind of social value that staff cannot efficiently substitute.

If true, outcomes must be observed beyond anecdotal warmth.

### H4 — Trust / responsibility / operating-boundary friction

The value exists on both sides, but repeated exchange remains constrained by:
- screening;
- duty scope;
- safety;
- emergency responsibility;
- insurance;
- house rules;
- activity verification;
- conflict resolution;
- exit/replacement.

This is attractive as an orchestration thesis.

But it is **not allowed to be assumed** merely because these tasks sound platform-like.

### H5 — Public-program economics

The exchange may work mainly because:
- public institutions tolerate below-market rent;
- volunteer accounting has social-policy value;
- managers absorb coordination cost;
- government/community infrastructure provides trust.

If H5 dominates, private normalized margin may be poor even when social value is high.

## 6. What would kill the formation

Kill or demote the formation if evidence shows any of the following:

- suitable spare capacity is rare or legally unusable;
- youth retention is weak once service burden and house rules are accounted for;
- companionship value is mostly narrative and not persistent;
- youth are implicitly substituting for professional care labor;
- public subsidy / institutional cross-subsidy is necessary to make exchange viable;
- current public platforms standardize the full trust/contract/routing layer;
- management cost consumes most capacity value;
- the same underlying structure does not regenerate enough exchange events;
- there is no private actor who benefits enough to pay for orchestration;
- the only viable unit is bespoke project management.
