# Xuzhou Field Validation Playbook

Status: `ACTIVE`

Last updated: 2026-09-10

## Purpose

The operator is physically in Xuzhou. Local access is an advantage only if field visits resolve explicit commercial unknowns.

Do not go out merely to "look around" or collect opinions.

Every visit must answer a falsifiable question and should, where appropriate, move from statements toward real economic commitment.

## 2. Universal interview rule

Ask about **recent behavior**, not hypothetical enthusiasm.

Weak:
> Would you use this service?

Strong:
> The last time this happened, what did you actually do?

Weak:
> Would you pay RMB 50?

Strong:
> What did you spend last time? If I offered this exact result next week at RMB 50, would you book now / leave a deposit?

Weak:
> Would your company / venue / institution sponsor this?

Strong:
> What did you spend last time to get this outcome? For this exact pilot, can you approve or commit RMB X now? If not, who can?

## 3. Universal questions

For almost every actor:

1. When did this problem last happen?
2. What did you do instead?
3. What did that cost in money, time, favors, risk or lost opportunity?
4. What was the worst part of the workaround?
5. Who made the spending decision?
6. What exact condition would make you transact with a new provider?
7. What would make you reject the transaction immediately?
8. Can the next step be a real booking, deposit, written sponsor commitment or paid pilot rather than another opinion?

## 4. Youth participant script — EXP-006

Target:
- roughly 20–35-year-old local students / workers who have participated in or actively sought after-work/weekend activities;
- prioritize people who can name a recent paid leisure/interest experience.

Resolve:
- actual desired outcome;
- direct willingness to pay;
- event trust / awkwardness barriers;
- whether willingness converts into a real deposit.

Ask:
1. What did you do last weekend / after work for leisure, social or interest activity?
2. What was the last activity you actually paid for? Price?
3. Have you joined a class, board-game group, city walk, workshop, sports group or social event?
4. What makes you avoid these events?
5. Which outcome matters most: skill, fun, meeting people, photos/content, stress relief, novelty?
6. Preferred group size / duration / time?
7. For this exact event, which price do you accept: RMB 29 / 49 / 79?
8. Will you place a real refundable deposit now at the selected price?

PASS evidence:
- Level 3 deposit, not verbal enthusiasm.

## 5. Venue / youth-shop script — EXP-006

Target:
- cafes, studios, craft shops, bookstores, board-game venues and other suitable public spaces with off-peak capacity.

Resolve:
- whether merchant traffic value can become a payer structure.

Ask:
1. Which recurring hours are genuinely underutilized?
2. How do you currently acquire new customers?
3. What does a typical promotion / platform discount / influencer visit cost?
4. Have you hosted activities before? What happened to revenue / customer unit price / repeat visits?
5. What are 8–12 qualified first-time visitors worth?
6. Will you commit RMB 200+ cash / minimum guarantee to this exact event?
7. If not, what revenue-share structure would you accept and what minimum contribution can you guarantee?
8. What measurable result would make you repeat next month?

Do not count free space as payer PASS. It is capability/resource evidence only.

## 6. Skills-to-income learner/provider script — EXP-007

Target:
- university students;
- recent graduates;
- skills-night-school / vocational-training learners;
- young flexible workers with one demonstrable deliverable capability.

Resolve:
- what capability is actually deliverable now;
- whether the actor lacks training or lacks access to real accepted work;
- minimum viable compensation;
- proof and reliability.

Ask:
1. What can you deliver today without more training?
2. Show one example / portfolio / test result.
3. Have you ever been paid for this exact capability?
4. What was the fee and who paid?
5. What real project would you accept this week?
6. Minimum fee?
7. What deadline / revision rules can you accept?
8. What work will you refuse or cannot safely/legally do?

A learner saying "I want projects" proves supply motivation, not demand.

## 7. Training institution / outcome sponsor script — EXP-007

Target:
- vocational / skills-training providers;
- project-based training operators;
- institutions whose funding, reputation or enrollment is affected by learner employment outcomes.

Resolve:
- whether stable-employment / project-outcome incentives translate into a willingness to sponsor a bounded real-work layer.

Ask:
1. For the last completed cohort, how many learners achieved stable employment / paid project outcomes?
2. What is the hardest step between course completion and a real accepted work outcome?
3. What staff time or budget do you currently spend on job matching, employer outreach, portfolio work or follow-up?
4. Does employment performance affect subsidy, future program allocation, reputation or enrollment?
5. Would a real bounded external project count as a useful learner outcome / portfolio artifact?
6. For one cohort of 3–5 learners, would you sponsor a fixed pilot that produces verified project outputs?
7. What exact deliverable / evidence would you need to approve RMB X?
8. Who has payment authority and what would make them reject it?

Do not count policy subsidy existence as payer PASS. Require an exact institution commitment.

## 8. Project buyer script — EXP-003 / EXP-007

Target:
- SMEs, merchants, OPC founders or other real buyers with recent bounded digital/data/content/research work.

Resolve:
- whether the buyer will pay for an accepted result rather than another employee / generic freelancer introduction.

Ask:
1. What small work did you need in the last 90 days but delay, assign reluctantly or outsource poorly?
2. What did it cost in cash or staff time?
3. Show one recent example if non-sensitive.
4. If the same outcome had fixed deliverables and acceptance criteria, would you buy it as a project?
5. What price would make the decision easy enough to test?
6. What makes you distrust an unknown student/freelancer/AI-assisted provider?
7. What proof / QA / revision rule would reduce the risk?
8. Can we define one real paid pilot now?

## 9. Pet-owner script — EXP-004

Target:
- owners who travel, work late, visit family or otherwise need temporary care;
- prioritize people who have actually paid for boarding / feeding / walking or asked friends for help.

Resolve:
- whether the main friction is price, availability or trust;
- whether managed verification creates enough payment value to compensate for home-access and provider-economics risk.

Ask:
1. Last absence: what happened to the pet?
2. Who cared for it?
3. What did you pay / owe as a favor?
4. Why did you choose boarding / friend / sitter?
5. Would you let an unknown sitter enter your home? Why / why not?
6. What proof would you require: ID, references, video, GPS/time, checklist, contract, emergency procedure?
7. What would make you reject the service immediately?
8. What price did you pay last time?
9. Would you pay extra for a verified/managed service? How much?
10. Would you place a real booking/deposit for a low-risk future need if the controls were defined?

Do not collect home access codes, keys, precise addresses or other unnecessary private data in public research records.

## 10. Pet-provider script — EXP-004

Target:
- students / flexible workers / pet owners / existing pet-service workers with actual animal experience.

Ask:
1. Have you cared for someone else's pet before?
2. Which animals/tasks will you accept or refuse?
3. Service radius?
4. Minimum fee per visit?
5. Holiday pricing?
6. Will you follow a written checklist?
7. Will you provide timestamped evidence?
8. What happens if the animal is ill, escapes or becomes aggressive?
9. Would you accept identity verification / reference checks?
10. What insurance / liability structure would you expect?

Kill signal:
- providers reject evidence/standards;
- travel time destroys low-ticket economics;
- home-access responsibility cannot be bounded.

## 11. Idle-asset owner / buyer probe — not yet promoted

Use only for lawful private-market discovery. Do not represent public-asset owners or act as an auction intermediary without authority.

Owner questions:
1. What asset is idle and for how long?
2. How have you tried to sell / rent / reuse it?
3. How many times was it listed or repriced?
4. What is the cost of keeping it idle?
5. What information do buyers repeatedly ask for?
6. Is inspection / logistics / lot size the blocking factor?
7. Would you pay for buyer-ready documentation / verification / qualified buyer discovery?

Buyer questions:
1. What used equipment/assets have you purchased recently?
2. How did you find them?
3. What stopped you buying other candidates?
4. What condition proof did you require?
5. How much travel / inspection effort did you spend?
6. Would a verified evidence pack materially change willingness to transact?

This is a probe, not authorization to create an asset marketplace.

## 12. Evidence levels

### Level 0 — statement
"I think I would use it."

### Level 1 — prior behavior
"I paid / asked / searched / used a workaround last month."

### Level 2 — concrete price acceptance
"For this exact scope I accept RMB X."

### Level 3 — commitment
Deposit, booking, written sponsor commitment or provider acceptance.

### Level 4 — completed transaction
Payment + delivery + acceptance.

### Level 5 — repeat / referral
The strongest early evidence.

## 14. Field discipline

- Do not lead the interviewee toward the desired answer.
- Do not treat friendliness as demand.
- Do not count the same actor twice as independent evidence.
- Record rejection reasons.
- A real refusal at a real price is more valuable than ten vague compliments.
- Public/free provision is an incumbent, not consumer payment evidence.
- A sponsor hypothesis does not pass until the sponsor commits real economic value.
- Do not collect sensitive personal information unless strictly necessary and lawfully handled.
- Never weaken safety / legal boundaries to obtain a transaction.

## 15. Governing principle

**Every trip should reduce one important uncertainty. Every real payment, refusal, delivery and repeat should update the model.**
