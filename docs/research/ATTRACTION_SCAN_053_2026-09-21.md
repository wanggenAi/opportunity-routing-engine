## Result

The scan tested six formation-diverse exception queues:

1. **Retail EDI chargeback recovery** — current brands pay weekly specialists to investigate deductions and recover dollars, but SPS Revenue Recovery already automates data retrieval, validity checks, evidence attachment, dispute submission/re-submission and outcome tracking.
2. **SaaS revenue-state drift** — a current company pays long-term to keep Stripe, HubSpot and product entitlements synchronized, but standard quote-to-cash and Stripe synchronization are rapidly moving into HubSpot Revenue Hub and iPaaS/entitlement platforms; the residual third-system mapping is client-specific integration engineering.
3. **Shipment exception resolution** — real recurring pain, but detection/classification and increasingly proactive actions are already a mature post-purchase/visibility category, while hard corrective actions require carriers, warehouses or customers.
4. **Freight invoice discrepancy recovery** — direct economic leakage exists, but exact products already match invoices, flag discrepancies, notify carriers and manage resolution.
5. **Cross-SaaS identity/access exceptions** — provisioning and deprovisioning are absorbed by SCIM/identity control planes; unresolved cases involve approvals, SoD, least privilege and security judgment.
6. **Travel disruption reissue queues** — ongoing paid queue exists, but fare rules, GDS commands, airline policy and customer communication keep expert judgment at the center.

## Structural finding

```text
DETECTED EXCEPTION
+
RECURRING PAID REMEDIATION
!=
UNOWNED CORRECTIVE-ACTION LAYER
```

When the remediation becomes standardized enough to be machine-executable, incumbent control planes tend to absorb it. The residual tail then concentrates the non-standard semantics: customer-specific mappings, security approvals, supplier coordination, fare rules or other expert judgment.
