## F1 — Public TLS certificate lifetime compression

This is a clean machine-infrastructure state change. CA/Browser Forum Baseline Requirements v2.3.0 cap subscriber certificate validity at 200 days for certificates issued from 15 March 2026, then 100 days from 15 March 2027 and 47 days from 15 March 2029. Domain/IP validation reuse also compresses from 200 to 100 and then 10 days.

That mechanically increases lifecycle events. DigiCert says shorter lifetimes increase renewal/reissuance frequency and that automation reduces manual effort and outage risk. Sectigo makes the same transition explicit and markets end-to-end CA-agnostic certificate lifecycle automation.

The important contradiction is therefore real but already resolved by existing control planes:

```text
MORE CERTIFICATE EVENTS
+ EXISTING DNS / ENDPOINT / CA RIGHTS
+ MANUAL OR FRAGMENTED OPERATIONS
!=
UNOWNED AUTOMATION EDGE
```

DigiCert Trust Lifecycle Manager/CertCentral, Sectigo Certificate Manager, ACME and cloud-native managers already cover the repeatable machine work. A new generic agent can call those surfaces but does not create DNS authority, private-key custody or deployment rights.

**Verdict:** demoted.

## F2 — U.S. A2L HVAC field transition

The A2L transition is a current trade-state change rather than a software category. EPA's technology-transition rule caps GWP for new residential/light-commercial AC and heat-pump systems at 700, and ACCA describes A2L refrigerants as the dominant new HVACR refrigerant class. ACCA's current training explicitly covers safe handling and the tools needed to work on A2L equipment.

The operational behavior is visible. Current 2026 industry reporting says A2L equipment represented 91% of HVAC distributor sales at the close of the 2025 cooling season. Distributor retrospectives describe inventory whiplash during the R-410A/R-454B transition, and manufacturer training now includes A2L-specific sensors, fault codes and remediation behavior. The temporary R-454B cylinder shortage has largely eased, so Scan 093 does **not** mislabel that shortage as a current unresolved white-space signal.

The remaining seam is not cleanly separable from physical execution. OEM compatibility, refrigerant supply, A2L-rated tools, site access, safe charging/leak work and contractor accountability all sit with existing manufacturers, distributors, training bodies and field technicians.

**Verdict:** demoted.

## F3 — EU e-commerce accessibility after the EAA

The European Accessibility Act has applied since 28 June 2025 to covered services including e-commerce. AccessibleEU also published EN 301 549 v4.1.1 in September 2026, adding WCAG 2.2 requirements while making clear that the new version is not yet the legal reference for EAA/WAD conformance until formally cited by the Commission.

A July 2026 vendor study attempted 170 large EU online stores and scored 107 reachable sites. It reported a critical barrier on 52.3% of scored sites and separately recorded 47 bot/CAPTCHA blocks and 16 JavaScript shells. That study is useful as a diagnostic current-state sample, **not** as a legal determination or statistically universal claim.

The partial flow is obvious: code scanners, CI checks and recurring monitoring can catch repeatable defects, while knowledgeable human evaluation is still needed for full accessibility assessment. W3C explicitly says no evaluation tool alone can determine whether a site meets accessibility standards. Deque and Level Access already provide recurring monitoring, engineering integration, remediation tracking and guided/manual workflows.

That means the persistent problem does not automatically create an unowned machine operator:

```text
CONTINUOUS CODE CHANGE
+ RECURRING ACCESSIBILITY DEFECTS
+ AUTOMATED TESTABILITY
!=
FULLY MACHINE-OWNED CONFORMANCE
```

**Verdict:** demoted.
