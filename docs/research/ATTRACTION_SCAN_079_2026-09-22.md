## Boundary

Scan 079 raises Scan 078 from “a distinct non-incumbent residual provider was actually paid” to:

```text
LIVE REPLACEMENT / PLATFORM
+ BUYER-AUTHORED SAME-OUTCOME RESIDUAL
+ EXECUTED RECURRING NON-INCUMBENT RESIDUAL PAYMENT
+ BUYER-AUTHORED EVIDENCE AFTER THAT PURCHASE
  THAT THE SAME OUTCOME STILL REMAINS UNRESOLVED
```

The scan stays mechanism-neutral. A router, multi-provider stack, abstraction layer or provider-switch requirement is **not** inherited.

## Formation notes

### F1 — Wright State + Turnitin AI detector

Wright State's Faculty Senate IT Committee records that the Turnitin AI detector returned under a one-year paid contract starting November 1, 2024 after faculty demand. The AI-detector feature cost $10,000 inside a $42,000 Turnitin contract, and more than 55,000 papers were processed before the committee's February 2025 report.

The temporal chain is unusually clean: money and live use come first; buyer-authored failure evidence comes later. CaTS testing still produced results ranging from very good to very bad, deliberate evasion remained a major weakness, short/non-prose content performed worse, and the committee recorded that tested AI-code detectors were not good.

That does **not** create an operator white space. Turnitin is the exact paid control surface, Copyleaks is another mature detector, and the buyer still needs policy, evidence review and due process before academic-integrity action.

### F2 — Woodburn + Flock Safety

Woodburn's December 2024 buyer packet defines Flock as a flat annual service subscription: $96,000 first year and $84,000 recurring. Council minutes record the 5-0 contract award and authorization to sign.

The February 2026 impact assessment provides rare post-purchase outcome evidence. Flock was used in 354 of 1,910 investigations; among 115 with stated outcomes, 29.57% explicitly recorded no contribution. The report also names gradual rollout, incomplete camera coverage and incompatibility with all investigation types as limiting factors.

This is real residual evidence after purchase, but it does not isolate a new digital operator edge. Physical sensing coverage is material, Flock is itself the end-to-end camera/software/cloud control surface, and the purchased outcome was assistance on applicable investigations rather than universal case resolution.

### F3 — Miami-Dade + Swiftly

Miami-Dade is the negative control required by the stricter causal rule. The County tested Swiftly as SaaS, awarded a multi-year contract and extended it while its aging CAD/AVL environment moved toward replacement.

Current buyer materials say Swiftly provides real-time positioning, ETAs, performance analytics and operational issue management, and that the software is being maintained for continuity until the future CAD/AVL absorbs the same functionality. That is recurring residual spend, but **not residual-layer failure**. Continuing core modernization cannot be re-labelled as failure of a complement the buyer says is working.

## No-repeat exclusions

Two superficially stronger chains were deliberately not admitted to the formation set:
- Richmond Police / Veritone Redact: strong paid-before/capacity-failure-after evidence, but it reuses the public-records/FOIA primary-signal family already covered in Scan 073.
- Aspen / AudioEye: strong deployed-overlay/remaining-accessibility-gap evidence, but it reuses the accessibility-remediation primary-signal family already covered in Scan 068.

This prevents the stronger temporal test from silently recycling old discovery ontology.

## Learning

```text
PAID RESIDUAL LAYER
+ BUYER-AUTHORED POST-PURCHASE FAILURE
!=
UNOWNED OPERATOR WHITE SPACE
```

Observed post-purchase failure can still be:
- the accuracy/coverage boundary of the exact mature product;
- a physical sensing or deployment boundary;
- a governance / due-process tail;
- or, in the negative control, not failure at all.

The first Scan 079 pass is therefore **not enough evidence to derive a provider-switch, multi-provider or other mechanism requirement**.
