## Search question

Scan 066 showed that two independent buyer problem signals still mix pain, internal workarounds and stated willingness with actual external spend.

Scan 067 therefore admitted only cases with at least two independent confirmed external money motions and then asked whether the paid unit itself was the same bounded, deterministic, repeatable and machine-verifiable execution unit.

## Result

Six formation-diverse paid flows were examined. None survived.

The decisive distinction is now not whether money moves, but **what the buyer is actually paying for**:

```text
TWO BUYERS PAY EXTERNAL PROVIDERS
!=
TWO BUYERS PAY FOR THE SAME UNBUNDLED ATOMIC EXECUTION UNIT
```

Two failure modes dominate:

1. the payment is a retainer or percentage that bundles deterministic work with judgment, context, accountability, support or risk transfer;
2. the paid unit really is atomic and machine-verifiable, in which case payroll and DMARC show that a mature software/control category often already owns it.

## Formations

### F1 — Outsourced medical billing / RCM

Current private-practice buyers report long-lived recurring billing-service payments around 6%-8% of collections.

The economic evidence passes strongly. The atomic-unit test does not. The purchased RCM service includes claims submission and payment posting, but also payer follow-up, denial management, appeals and account responsibility. AdvancedMD currently sells the same full-service RCM layer at 4%-8% of collections.

**Closure:** real recurring money; mature category plus payer-specific judgment/accountability bundle.

### F2 — Live / hybrid virtual receptionist

Independent law-firm buyers pay recurring external providers: one reports roughly $700/month for Ruby overflow, another reports paying under $2,000/month for Lex.

Call pickup, field capture and routing are machine-amenable, but the paid service includes intake quality, empathy, bilingual handling, scheduling, payment collection, lead qualification and conversion responsibility. Ruby already packages this control surface and is adding AI assistance.

**Closure:** real recurring money; service bundle and mature human/AI receptionist category.

### F3 — Monthly bookkeeping / reconciliation

Independent restaurant operators report recurring payments including $900/month for payables and reconciliation and $715/month for broader bookkeeping.

Reconciliation itself is objectively checkable, but the observed payment is not priced to that atomic unit. QuickBooks Live already provides monthly transaction categorization and account reconciliation with certified bookkeepers. The residual retainer buys classification judgment, reporting, tax context and accountability.

**Closure:** real recurring money; atomic reconciliation absorbed, broader payment is a judgment bundle.

### F4 — Podcast editing

Independent podcast buyers report real per-episode/per-hour payments, including roughly $30-$35/hour and $50-$140 per episode.

This is stronger than a generic monthly retainer because payment can attach to an episode. But standard cleanup—silence trimming, filler-word removal and transcription—is already automated by current editing tools such as Descript. The valuable residual is pacing, story selection, taste and seamless contextual editing.

**Closure:** unit-priced money exists; deterministic subunit is productized and the residual acceptance test is subjective.

### F5 — Small-business payroll

Current small-business buyers pay accountants or payroll platforms repeatedly. One S-Corp owner reports $125/month rising to $200/month for outsourced payroll; other businesses report multi-year Gusto usage.

Payroll has exceptionally clean deterministic physics, but those same physics have already produced dense software categories. Gusto currently sells payroll, tax filing and payments from a recurring base fee plus per-person pricing.

**Closure:** money and machine-verifiable output both pass; pre-category white space fails.

### F6 — DMARC monitoring

Current MSP/sysadmin users explicitly pay recurring external providers for DMARC aggregation and monitoring, including a reported EUR 80 package for 120 domains and a migration from a higher-priced EasyDMARC renewal to a lower-cost competitor.

This is the cleanest atomic execution unit in the scan: ingest reports, aggregate, classify and alert. It is also already commoditized. URIports currently sells automated DMARC/DNS monitoring from low single-digit monthly tiers and exposes MSP/API features; multiple competing and free/basic options exist.

**Closure:** atomic paid unit passes; dense low-cost SaaS control surface kills operator differentiation.

## Learning

Scan 067 removes another false-positive class: **confirmed external money motion by itself**.

The stronger next search boundary is payment attributable to the atomic execution unit itself. Scan 068 should require two independent buyers to pay for the same **unbundled** unit, with unit/task-level price attribution and objective machine-verifiable acceptance. A broad service retainer, percentage-of-revenue contract or provider subscription is not enough unless the money can be causally assigned to the atomic unit being evaluated.
