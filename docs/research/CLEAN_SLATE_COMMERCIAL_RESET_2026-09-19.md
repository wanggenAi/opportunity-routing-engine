## 3. Clean-slate value-flow logic

The engine starts from two real sides that already exist.

### Side A — underused energy / pain

A person, group, asset or capability has:
- unused time;
- underused skill;
- idle capacity;
- need for income / utilization / access;
- willingness to perform a bounded task if a credible task appears.

### Side B — current paid outcome need

Another actor already has:
- a concrete task;
- a current cost, delay, missing outcome or workload;
- willingness to pay for a bounded result now;
- an outcome that can be checked and accepted.

The business begins only when the two can be connected with low enough friction.

```text
SIDE A: UNDERUSED CAPACITY / INCOME NEED
+
SIDE B: CURRENT PAID OUTCOME
+
MISSING TRUST / DISCOVERY / PACKAGING / ACCEPTANCE EDGE
=
DIRECTLY ROUTABLE VALUE FLOW
```

## 4. Money must be visible before architecture

A preferred clean-slate candidate can answer, in plain language:

1. Who pays?
2. What exact result are they paying for?
3. Who can deliver it?
4. How is the result accepted?
5. When does money settle?
6. Why does the operator remain useful after buyer and provider know each other?

If these cannot be answered with a short path, do not hide the problem behind policy, identity, platform, ontology or long-term ecosystem language.

## 5. Operator role

The operator is the **connector / organizer / acceptance layer**.

The operator should not become:
- permanent coder;
- permanent photographer;
- permanent translator;
- permanent salesperson;
- permanent delivery worker.

The operator may initially perform small manual coordination to prove the flow, but the delivered work should be performed by routable external capacity.

The operator's legitimate value may include:
- finding a real task;
- converting vague need into a bounded work unit;
- selecting a suitable provider;
- setting acceptance criteria;
- reducing trust/search cost;
- coordinating exceptions;
- confirming delivery;
- settling payment;
- replacing a failed provider when necessary.

## 6. Preferred first-flow characteristics

Prefer:
- already-observed paid task;
- small ticket / bounded scope;
- short sales cycle;
- clear deliverable;
- clear acceptance;
- low regulatory burden;
- no inventory;
- no major capex;
- no required certification before the first test;
- no need to build a marketplace first;
- provider can be replaced;
- buyer can transact once before committing long term;
- operator can earn from the first completed unit.

Do not make every item a hard universal gate, but treat major violations as warning signs.

## 7. Kill patterns

Strongly deprioritize a route when the first money requires:
- obtaining an identity/qualification unrelated to the actual value;
- building a platform before one transaction;
- waiting for policy subsidy;
- educating a buyer that they should want the outcome;
- multi-month institutional relationship building;
- capital-intensive inventory;
- the operator personally delivering every unit;
- unclear acceptance or settlement.
