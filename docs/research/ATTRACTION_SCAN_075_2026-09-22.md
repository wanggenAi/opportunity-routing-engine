## Boundary

This scan raises the buyer-evidence floor after two procurement/renewal passes.

A useful seed now requires the buyer to move away from a **paid incumbent** — replacement, non-renewal, exit or competitive displacement — because a documented operational outcome remains unresolved, with a funded replacement path.

Price-only switching, end-of-support/deprecation alone, ordinary expiry, policy-only recompete and persistent workaround without actual spend movement are excluded. No mechanism shape is inherited.

## Cross-scan conclusion

The stronger signal changes what is known:

```text
PAID INCUMBENT
→ DOCUMENTED OPERATIONAL FAILURE / CAPABILITY GAP
→ BUYER ACTUALLY MOVES REPLACEMENT BUDGET
→ STRONGER THAN RENEWAL
→ BUT MONEY MOSTLY MOVES HORIZONTALLY TO ANOTHER COMPLETE PLATFORM / INTEGRATOR
```

So Scan 075 validates that buyer dissatisfaction can be real and expensive without leaving a founder-independent execution layer.

The important remaining question is no longer “will the buyer replace the incumbent?” It is:

> after the replacement is already installed, does the buyer separately keep paying another party on a recurring basis for the **same unresolved outcome**?

That is a stronger test for an unowned residual value layer.
