## Current structural hypotheses

The research evidence supports further observation of several structures, but none is an ObservedPattern or opportunity yet.

### 1. Repeated household service demand may be shifting toward trusted recurring fulfillment

National and Jiangsu policy/data show service consumption and home-service supply being expanded, while recurring-care demand is becoming more visible. The counterevidence is equally important: prepaid cards and long-duration advance-payment models still generate significant consumer risk. Therefore the working mechanism is **recurring demand + trusted fulfillment**, not `prepayment = good subscription economics`.

### 2. Small-enterprise pressure can coexist with improving aggregate demand

August manufacturing new orders improved while small-enterprise PMI remained below the expansion threshold. The longitudinal OSOME survey also provides a reusable observation surface for revenue, margin, cash-flow and confidence. This may eventually expose underused capabilities or demand-routing frictions, but aggregate divergence is not proof of an opportunity.

### 3. Retail formats are polarizing rather than uniformly shrinking or growing

Convenience stores and supermarkets outperform several traditional formats in national statistics, while industry data show regional concentration, online-share growth and unit-economics pressure. The correct hypothesis is structural channel reallocation, not `convenience store growth = open stores`.

### 4. Consumer behavior appears more selective, not simply upgraded or downgraded

Service/experience categories and policy-supported goods can grow while overall retail growth remains modest and job-security concerns remain high. The research question is where households still perceive sufficiently high utility/value to spend, and what categories they defer, substitute or downgrade.

### 5. Elder-care capacity is being reconfigured across payer, provider and service boundaries

Jiangsu is using consumer subsidies to expand home-service supply and explicitly bring home-service, property and rehabilitation providers into the service pool. Medical/elder-care policy also encourages primary and lower-level medical institutions to develop long-term care capability. In Xuzhou, long-term-care insurance already has a large service network and formal operating/administration interfaces. This is a promising structure for further actor-level observation, but payer identity, provider economics, service quality, capacity and repeated settlement must remain separate evidence questions.

## Counterevidence preserved

- Prepaid/annual-card mechanics can increase consumer exposure to service shrinkage, refund difficulty and merchant failure.
- Convenience-store growth coexists with traffic, ticket-size and single-store productivity pressure.
- Chinese consumers can remain relatively optimistic while simultaneously becoming more selective and job-security-sensitive.
- Policy encouragement and institutional expansion do not prove consumer willingness, unit economics or provider spare capacity.
