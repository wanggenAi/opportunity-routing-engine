`ATTRACTION_SCAN_003-F1 — AI CUSTOM-SERVICE ACCEPTANCE RAIL`

The retained structure is not:
- another AI development shop;
- another freelance marketplace;
- another generic Agent evaluation platform;
- a manual project-management / QA consulting service.

The hypothesized transition is:

```
VAGUE AI SERVICE REQUEST
→ PROVIDER DEMO / ITERATION
→ SUBJECTIVE "DONE / NOT DONE"
→ REVISION / REFUND / TAIL-PAYMENT DISPUTE
```

into:

```
FROZEN TRANSACTION-SIZED ACCEPTANCE OBJECT
→ EXECUTABLE / REPRODUCIBLE TESTS
→ VERSIONED DELIVERY EVIDENCE
→ PASS / FAIL / RETEST / CLOSEOUT
```

Current evidence establishes:
- very large and fast-growing current AI-service transaction activity on a consumer/light-business marketplace;
- an economically meaningful higher-ticket tail in AI deployment/technical services;
- provider-side pain when acceptance remains subjective;
- buyer-side and enterprise-side difficulty defining what “accepted” means for probabilistic AI systems;
- mature generic escrow/dispute marketplaces and mature formal AI testing/evaluation products.

Therefore the only residual worth testing is whether a **low-friction, transaction-sized acceptance contract + executable evidence pack** exists between informal marketplace dispute handling and expensive enterprise testing/consulting.

That residual is not yet proven.

## Retained formation — ATTRACTION_SCAN_003-F1

### Working title

**AI Custom-Service Acceptance Rail**

### Actor A — AI service provider

Examples:
- AI coding / site-building provider;
- workflow automation provider;
- Agent builder;
- AI deployment / integration provider.

### A-side temptation

Get paid against a bounded definition of “done,” reduce subjective revision loops, prevent scope drift and make tail-payment closeout less dependent on argument.

### A-side evidence

Current AI-service supply is large and growing.

A current seller case describes an Agent customization project where:
- work continued for weeks;
- there was no sufficiently quantified written acceptance standard;
- the buyer later rejected the result based on subjective expectations;
- a 12,000 RMB tail payment was disputed;
- the seller concluded that quantitative acceptance criteria, revision caps and staged payment should be agreed in advance.

Truth state: `CURRENT BEHAVIORAL MOTION / ECONOMIC PAIN`.

### Actor B — buyer / purchaser

Examples:
- small business buying an AI workflow;
- entrepreneur buying an AI site/agent;
- enterprise team buying a bounded AI implementation.

### B-side temptation

Know before paying whether the delivered system performs the agreed business task, under the agreed data/version/environment, with observable evidence rather than a persuasive demo.

### B-side evidence

Current procurement and delivery guidance repeatedly treats AI acceptance as difficult because:
- output is probabilistic;
- business outcomes matter more than chat quality;
- tool calls / workflow execution must be checked;
- permissions, logs, cost and human handoff may matter;
- fixed-feature software acceptance logic does not map cleanly to AI systems.

Truth state: `CURRENT REPEATED ACCEPTANCE PROBLEM`.

## Structural friction hypothesis

The key friction is not primarily “AI quality is hard to measure.”

It is:

> the buyer and provider often do not freeze the same measurable acceptance object **before** work and payment risk accumulate.

For a small/mid-sized custom AI transaction, the acceptance object may need:

- exact input/output examples;
- frozen test set;
- environment/model/tool versions;
- workflow steps;
- tool/API execution requirements;
- permission boundaries;
- deterministic checks where possible;
- probabilistic thresholds where necessary;
- latency/cost limits where relevant;
- failure / fallback behavior;
- evidence/log requirements;
- revision boundary;
- retest rule.

The hypothesis is that this transaction object can be lighter than enterprise QA/consulting and more objective than marketplace dispute mediation.

Truth state: `EVIDENCED FRICTION / RESIDUAL EDGE UNPROVEN`.

## The barbell kill test

Current market evidence creates a severe barbell risk.

### Low-ticket side

A large share of AI marketplace work appears side-hustle / low-value.

If the underlying job is hundreds or low thousands of RMB:
- a paid independent acceptance layer may cost too much relative to transaction value;
- platform dispute handling may be “good enough”;
- buyer/provider may not tolerate setup friction.

### High-ticket side

At tens/hundreds of thousands of RMB:
- procurement teams can demand written SOW/acceptance criteria;
- enterprise AI platforms provide evaluation;
- formal testing/consulting vendors can be hired;
- bespoke technical judgment becomes economically tolerable.

### Required surviving middle

F1 survives only if a meaningful middle band exists where:

```
TRANSACTION VALUE HIGH ENOUGH
TO PAY FOR OBJECTIVE ACCEPTANCE

AND

FORMAL ENTERPRISE QA / CONSULTING
IS TOO HEAVY OR TOO EXPENSIVE

AND

ACCEPTANCE CAN BE GENERATED / EXECUTED
WITH LOW MANUAL EXPERT LABOR
```

Truth state: `DECISIVE UNKNOWN`.

## Connection pressure

A-side:
- providers want bounded scope and reliable closeout.

B-side:
- buyers want confidence and evidence before final payment/acceptance.

Marketplace/payment side:
- disputes become cheaper if an agreed acceptance object exists before work begins.

The parties are already transacting; the pressure is concentrated inside an existing transaction.

Result: `MULTILATERAL PRESSURE EVIDENCED`.

## Attraction Brief

### A-SIDE TEMPTATION

“I want the buyer to agree before I start what counts as finished, so I can avoid endless revisions and subjective refusal to pay.”

### B-SIDE TEMPTATION

“I want to know that the AI system actually performs the agreed workflow under reproducible tests before I release final payment.”

### OPERATOR TEMPTATION

High **only if** the operator owns a reusable acceptance artifact/protocol and automated evidence workflow.

Low if the operator becomes:
- project manager;
- bespoke requirements consultant;
- manual tester;
- domain expert;
- dispute mediator.

### OPERATOR NON-LABOR TEST

The formation fails if every transaction requires the operator to:
- interview both sides deeply;
- manually write the acceptance spec;
- create bespoke test data;
- judge subjective outputs;
- run tests manually;
- mediate disagreement;
- certify domain correctness.

It can survive only if a narrow category brief can be transformed into a frozen acceptance pack quickly, with most evaluation executable/reproducible.

Current result: `UNPROVEN`.

### CONNECTION WOW

Transform:

`“给我做个智能体 / 自动化” → Demo → “感觉不对” → 无限改 / 拒尾款`

into:

`Brief → Frozen acceptance contract → Executable tests → Evidence → Close/retest`.

The value jump is in making “done” legible before conflict.

### ACTIVATION FRICTION

Key unresolved friction:
- willingness to define acceptance before work;
- quality of initial requirements;
- non-deterministic outputs;
- model/version drift;
- external API variability;
- domain-specific correctness;
- test-data ownership/privacy;
- platform compatibility;
- liability if the acceptance pack misses a failure;
- transaction value versus acceptance cost.

### SELF-PROPULSION

Plausible but unproven.

Repeated accepted transactions could create:
- category-specific acceptance templates;
- reusable test primitives;
- benchmark libraries;
- failure-mode libraries;
- evidence formats;
- better initial requirement questions;
- lower future setup cost.

But this only compounds if those assets transfer across projects without heavy domain-specific rewriting.

## Control-right / permission check

Unlike Scan 002-F1, the relevant transaction rights are relatively concentrated:
- buyer can define desired outcome and acceptance;
- provider can agree scope;
- marketplace/payment rail can bind payment/dispute state.

There is no obvious need for dozens of local third-party permissions.

Result: `CONTROL RIGHTS RELATIVELY CONCENTRATED`.

This improves routability but does not prove demand for a separate layer.

## Decisive next questions

1. Does a viable **middle ticket-size band** exist between low-value gig work and formal enterprise projects?
2. Can a project brief become a usable acceptance pack in minutes rather than consulting hours?
3. Which 1–2 AI-service categories have mostly executable acceptance with low domain judgment?
4. Can both buyer and provider agree to one frozen acceptance object before work begins?
5. Does this materially reduce rework, refunds or unpaid tail payments enough to justify cost/friction?
6. Can the evidence pack plug into existing order/milestone/payment/dispute flows without replacing the marketplace?
7. Do current acceptance/PoC consulting services already solve the exact middle band?
8. Can provider-side sharing of the acceptance pack create acquisition without founder cold sales?
9. Can the layer remain measurement/evidence rather than assuming product-performance liability?
10. How much of evaluation must be probabilistic or human-judged, and does that destroy non-labor economics?

Until those are answered:
