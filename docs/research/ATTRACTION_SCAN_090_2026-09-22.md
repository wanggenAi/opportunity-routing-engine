## F1 — paid cross-SaaS workflow automation

Current participants explicitly report paying Zapier for production workflows:
- one April 2026 Professional user had been on the paid plan for more than a year and saw their bill rise from roughly $30 to $160 after adding AI nodes;
- one May 2026 Team user says they paid $69/month and describes working lead-intake, digest and enrichment flows spanning multiple services before migrating them.

This is direct payment for moving data and triggering actions across existing applications.

Closure:
- Zapier itself is already exact infrastructure for this coordination layer, with 9,000+ integrations;
- app connections remain authorization-bound to each upstream system;
- pricing/task economics can create dissatisfaction, but that does not create an unowned generic automation layer;
- Make/n8n and other mature alternatives already compete on the same coordination surface.

Verdict: **demoted**.

## F2 — paid cross-site, cross-device credential coordination

2026 participants explicitly report active or renewing 1Password subscriptions and continued willingness to pay despite price increases. The value is credential storage, sync and use across many websites, browsers and devices.

Closure:
- 1Password and peer password managers already own this exact secure middle layer;
- destination websites retain authoritative authentication, MFA, passkey and recovery control;
- secure secret handling creates deterministic boundaries that a generic agent should not bypass;
- switching caused by price pressure is competitor substitution, not evidence of an unowned coordination role.

Verdict: **demoted**.

## F3 — paid multi-calendar/task/scheduling aggregation

A September 2026 participant explicitly says they pay $90/year for Fantastical; another paying user in the same discussion says the cost is worthwhile for organizational sanity and values the Todoist integration. A January 2026 long-time customer reports paying annually since subscriptions began.

The product coordinates Google, Microsoft 365/Exchange, iCloud, Todoist, CalDAV and other accounts in one planning surface.

Closure:
- Fantastical itself is already the exact paid coordination incumbent;
- Calendar Sets, tasks and scheduling absorb the cross-provider organization layer;
- underlying providers retain systems of record, sharing policies and provider-specific write restrictions;
- some operations still must be performed on the provider website, but that residual is provider-rights bound rather than an unowned generic scheduling layer.

Verdict: **demoted**.

## Two-pass learning

Scan 089 and Scan 090 now provide six formation-diverse observations across:
- award travel;
- personal finance;
- fitness data;
- cross-SaaS automation;
- credentials;
- calendar/task scheduling.

Across all six, users demonstrably pay for coordination. Across all six, exact mature paid products already capture the middle layer and/or decisive data/action rights remain with underlying incumbents.

The project should therefore stop treating “coordination payment exists” as a sufficient attraction signal.

The next stronger reality signal is **residual failure after payment plus a second real economic cost**.

Scan 091 should look for:
1. users already paying an existing coordination product;
2. a recurring, high-value incompleteness that remains after payment;
3. at least two unaffiliated paying users independently bearing a second observable cost to finish the same outcome — either another paid service/provider or repeated explicitly quantified manual work;
4. no single current control surface already owning the repair path.

This searches for paid pain *inside* an already monetized coordination market rather than merely proving the market exists.
