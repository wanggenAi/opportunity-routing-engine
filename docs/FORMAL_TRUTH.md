# Formal Truth

Last updated: 2026-09-08

This document is the project's commercial source of truth. It separates observations, hypotheses, unknowns, and decisions. No attractive narrative may override missing evidence.

## 1. Mission

Continuously detect opportunity created by social and market change, identify the actors affected, convert repeated friction into explicit need hypotheses, identify the real beneficiary and payer, route viable capabilities/resources, and test whether the mismatch can become a real transaction.

## 2. Core operating model

```text
OBSERVE ACTORS
→ DETECT CHANGE
→ DEFINE NEED
→ IDENTIFY BENEFICIARY / PAYER
→ VALIDATE PAYMENT BEHAVIOR
→ ROUTE CAPABILITY
→ TRANSACT
→ LEARN
```

The project is deliberately industry-agnostic and actor-agnostic.

It may discover opportunities among individuals, households, students, workers, elderly people, caregivers, merchants, enterprises, manufacturers, institutions, communities, overseas actors, or holders of idle resources.

## 3. Actor-first truth

The system must not default to enterprises as the demand side.

For every opportunity, explicitly identify:

- `NEED_ACTOR` — who experiences the friction or unmet need;
- `BENEFICIARY` — who receives the result;
- `PAYER` — who has incentive and ability to pay;
- `CAPABILITY_PROVIDER` — who/what can solve it;
- `ORCHESTRATOR` — who structures and coordinates the transaction;
- `RESOURCE_OWNER` — where relevant, who controls an asset, inventory, time, space, data, access or other resource.

These roles may be the same actor or several different actors.

Examples of valid structures:

- individual pays for individual service;
- parent pays for child outcome;
- adult child pays for elderly parent service;
- enterprise pays for student/freelancer project output;
- institution pays for resident/student/public benefit;
- consumer pays a merchant;
- one individual pays another to access idle capability/resource;
- supplier pays to reach demand;
- advertiser/sponsor pays while end user receives the service.

## 4. What we are NOT building

- A generic trend-report business
- A social-media sentiment toy
- A crawler whose value is merely collecting public information
- A raw lead-list resale business
- A general marketplace before transaction density exists
- A traditional intermediary that only exchanges contact details
- A system that treats complaints as proof of willingness to pay
- A system that assumes the sufferer must be the payer
- A system that privileges B2B merely because enterprise budgets are easier to observe
- A system that treats LLM confidence as commercial truth

## 5. Current strategic thesis

Social development continually creates temporary imbalances between actors, needs and capabilities. Commercial opportunities may appear when one or more of the following occur:

- a group develops a new need before supply adapts;
- one group has idle time/skill/resources while another has scarcity;
- a household or individual need is real but the payer is another family member or institution;
- people keep the desired outcome but reject the old price, format, commitment or channel;
- supply exists but discovery, trust, timing, geography, coordination or verification blocks exchange;
- a new technology lowers the cost of solving an old problem;
- economic pressure moves spending from premium solutions toward substitutes, reuse, automation, sharing, repair, small trials or project-based services;
- demographic, employment, family, regulatory, geographic or cultural changes create new coordination needs;
- assets, skills, time, space or inventory remain idle while another actor experiences shortage.

The engine's potential value is to detect these mismatches early, define the real transaction correctly, route suitable capability, reduce risk/friction and learn from outcomes.

## 6. Opportunity truth gates

A candidate is not commercially qualified until evidence exists across:

1. `PAIN` — a real, consequential need/friction exists;
2. `FREQUENCY` — it repeats or affects enough economic/social activity;
3. `PAYER_CLARITY` — payer is identifiable and has incentive/ability to pay;
4. `PAYMENT_EVIDENCE` — material spending or costly workaround behavior exists;
5. `SUPPLY` — realistic capability/resource can solve it;
6. `TRANSACTIONABILITY` — scope, price, trust, delivery, verification and responsibility can be bounded;
7. `DEFENSIBILITY` — repeated execution can accumulate proprietary data, trust, workflow, network or decision advantage.

`PAYER_CLARITY`, `PAYMENT_EVIDENCE`, and `TRANSACTIONABILITY` are hard gates before expensive build-out.

## 7. Strong demand evidence

Higher-value evidence includes:

- real purchases or paid services;
- repeated small payments by individuals/households;
- subscriptions, memberships, bookings or reservations;
- marketplace tasks and service orders;
- procurement/RFQ/tender activity;
- companies hiring specifically to solve the problem;
- parents/family members paying for another person's outcome;
- customers paying for imperfect substitutes;
- costly travel, waiting, manual coordination or repeated help-seeking;
- explicit budget allocation;
- repeated failed attempts to solve the problem;
- switching toward substitute products/services;
- observed secondary-market, rental, repair, sharing, or gig transactions.

Complaints, surveys, social posts, search volume and macro indicators may generate hypotheses but are not sufficient alone.

## 8. Market structures

The engine must remain open to:

- `B2B`
- `B2C`
- `C2C`
- `C2B`
- `SPONSORED / THIRD-PARTY-PAYER`
- `MULTI-SIDED`

The evaluation unit is not an industry label. It is an **actor need + payer + capability + transaction structure**.

## 9. Opportunity status vocabulary

Use one of:

- `SIGNAL`
- `HYPOTHESIS`
- `EVIDENCED`
- `TRANSACTION_TEST`
- `VALIDATED`
- `REPEATABLE`
- `SCALE_CANDIDATE`
- `REJECTED`
- `DORMANT`

Never promote a state merely because an LLM predicts success.

## 10. Current truth status

The method itself is unproven. The immediate objective is to test whether it can repeatedly produce transaction-worthy opportunities across both individual and organizational actors.

## 13. Hard unknowns

1. Can the engine detect useful opportunities earlier or more accurately than ordinary manual research?
2. Can it distinguish loud complaints from actual payment behavior reliably?
3. Can it discover third-party payers when the need actor will not pay directly?
4. Can scoring compare B2B, B2C, C2C and sponsored structures fairly?
5. Which Xuzhou actor groups show the strongest combination of pain, payment and routable capability?
6. Can capability routing reduce price, time, quality, trust, search or coordination friction enough to earn a margin?
7. Which personal/household categories are attractive after acquisition, trust and support costs?
8. Can repeated experiments produce proprietary learning rather than generic public knowledge?
9. When does a human-managed transaction flow justify platform automation?
10. Which categories create regulatory, licensing, labor, privacy, medical, financial or safety constraints that must be excluded or handled by qualified providers?

## 14. Truth discipline

- `UNKNOWN != PASS`.
- Need actor does not imply payer.
- Complaint does not imply purchase.
- Macro trend evidence is not automatically micro-level purchase evidence.
- Correlation is not a causal business mechanism.
- Market existence does not prove our ability to acquire users/payers.
- One transaction validates possibility, not repeatability.
- Code completion is never evidence of commercial success.
