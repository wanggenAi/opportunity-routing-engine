# Potential Flow Field Packet 005 — Cross-Cultural Market Context

Status: `PARALLEL_DIRECT_ACTOR_PROBE_SENT / NO_VALIDATION_YET`

Formation: `PF-INTL-STUDENT-MARKET-CONTEXT-014`

This packet does **not** create a translation business, an international-student labor pool, an export consultancy or a marketplace. It tests whether a narrow pre-market validation edge exists after subtracting the substantial formal export-service infrastructure already available in Jiangsu.

## Observed gradient

### Context-resource side

Xuzhou has a real, reachable international-student population. Public 2026 evidence includes roughly 250 international students at Xuzhou University of Technology and more than 300 new international students participating in orientation at China University of Mining and Technology.

Potential resource, not assumed availability:

- native-language intuition;
- home-country consumer interpretation;
- everyday platform/channel familiarity;
- first-contact and communication norms;
- ability to flag wording that is literally correct but locally unnatural;
- lived understanding of obvious cultural or market-assumption errors.

This is **not** authority to provide legal, tax, regulatory, investment or professional market-entry advice.

### Enterprise side

Xuzhou and Jiangsu firms are actively expanding overseas, including Southeast Asia. At the same time, Jiangsu is building an extensive formal overseas-service system spanning legal, tax, finance, compliance, exhibitions, overseas service points, chambers and professional providers.

Therefore generic “help companies go overseas” is already heavily organized and is not the target.

## External precedent

A 2026 Shanghai university practice case documents Thai international students supporting Chinese exhibitors at a Thailand manufacturing exhibition. Their bilingual and local-context knowledge helped address language barriers, cultural differences and unfamiliarity with local market rules.

This proves only that the resource can matter in practice. It does **not** prove a standalone commercial market or that Xuzhou actors want the same structure.

## Narrow missing-edge question

Before an SME/OPC pays for formal consulting, travels, exhibits or establishes a local operation, does it repeatedly encounter **small but decision-relevant market-context uncertainties** that are too narrow for a conventional consulting engagement?

Examples to test rather than assume:

- “This translation is technically correct, but would a real local buyer say it this way?”
- “Would this first outreach message feel normal or spammy in that market?”
- “Do buyers in this category actually use the channel we assume they use?”
- “Does this product claim create a different local interpretation than we expect?”
- “Is our assumed first-contact sequence culturally normal?”

The contribution must remain factual/contextual and bounded. It must not drift into regulated professional advice.

## Existing routes that must be subtracted

- translation agencies;
- professional market-research firms;
- export consultants;
- overseas service stations;
- chambers of commerce / CCPIT;
- cross-border e-commerce service providers;
- lawyers, accountants, tax and compliance providers;
- local distributors or agents;
- standard internships and work-study;
- university international-exchange programs.

If one of these already solves the same micro-check cheaply and quickly, this formation should be killed rather than duplicated.

## Legal gate — hard constraint

International students are **not an available labor pool by default**.

Under current national rules, a student holding a study residence permit who undertakes off-campus work-study or internship must have school approval and apply for the relevant residence-permit annotation covering the unit/location/period. Without that annotation, off-campus work-study or internship is not allowed.

Therefore the current phase is limited to voluntary research interviews.

`INTERVIEW != WORK`

`RESOURCE_EXISTS != LEGALLY_AVAILABLE_FOR_COMMERCIAL_DELIVERY`

No paid task, enterprise placement or commercial delivery may be arranged until an institution confirms a lawful structure and required immigration formalities are satisfied.

## Student-side interview card

Ask only:

- country/market they know first-hand, at a broad level;
- one recent example where local context changed the interpretation of otherwise correct language;
- what categories they can judge confidently;
- what they cannot judge;
- what would require a professional instead;
- whether they have ever informally helped a Chinese peer/teacher/team with such context;
- whether current university social-practice/internship mechanisms already organize this contribution;
- willingness for a bounded 15–30 minute context check **in principle**, without treating this as work authorization.

Do not collect passport, visa/residence numbers, private contacts, financial distress, ethnicity/religion/politics or other sensitive personal information.

## Enterprise-side interview card

Ask for the most recent real overseas-market decision where uncertainty existed before heavy commitment:

- target country and broad customer type;
- exact small uncertainty;
- what decision depended on it;
- current workaround;
- whether a translator, chamber, consultant, local agent or existing service route was tried;
- why that route did or did not fit;
- what objective fact would have changed the decision;
- maximum useful turnaround time;
- whether a 15–30 minute local-context check would materially alter a real next step.

Do not ask for customer lists, contracts, source code, pricing secrets or regulated compliance advice.

## Promotion gate

Do not pair actors until all are true:

- `>= 3/5` enterprise actors show the same category of low-level pre-market uncertainty after current routes are considered;
- `>= 3/5` international-student actors show first-hand relevant context and clear uncertainty/refusal boundaries;
- the contribution is more than literal translation but less than professional consulting;
- at least one lawful participation route is confirmed by a university/institution before compensation or delivery;
- acceptance can be written objectively;
- at least one enterprise actor makes a real commitment after the legal gate is resolved.

## Kill conditions

Kill if:

- professional translation/export services already solve the same micro-check cheaply;
- the student contribution is too anecdotal or inconsistent for decision use;
- value requires legal/tax/compliance expertise;
- the model needs students to work without proper approval/annotation;
- it relies on cheap or unpaid foreign-student labor;
- coordination cost exceeds the value of the micro-check;
- enterprises do not change real decisions or make commitments.

## Parallel scan closures from the same pass

### Generic merchant new-media operation

Do not promote. Xuzhou agricultural wholesale-market merchants do show a public digital-skill gap, but the market already supplies official traffic, routine livestreaming and practical training, while commercial Douyin/e-commerce agencies and official service-provider mechanisms are abundant.

Reopen only around a repeated **post-training / post-agency** failure that is below ordinary agency economics or has a distinct acceptance/coordination problem.

### Idle industrial land / old factory space

Not P0. Current Xuzhou evidence shows active public repurposing, while core blockers include debt, unclear title and legacy liabilities. These are high-permission, multi-party problems.

### Generic SME export support

Kill as generic route. Jiangsu already has dense formal overseas-service infrastructure. Only the narrow pre-market context edge above remains worth cheap falsification.
