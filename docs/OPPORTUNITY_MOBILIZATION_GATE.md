## 1. Purpose

A candidate may be real, reachable and still be commercially unattractive for the current operator.

After reality has established an evidenced structural friction, connection pressure and a narrow inter-node missing edge, the system must ask:

> **If the missing edge is opened, will both sides already want to move through it with little founder push?**

This is `MOBILIZATION POTENTIAL`.

It is not market size, idea attractiveness or founder excitement. It is an evidence-bound estimate of how much actor energy is already available to activate the route.

The operator's preferred role is:

> **build the bridge; do not carry every actor across it.**

## 2. Core distinctions

```text
REALITY VALIDITY
!=
MOBILIZATION POTENTIAL
!=
MARKET VALIDATION
!=
REPEATABLE BUSINESS
```

```text
DEMAND PAIN
!=
DEMAND PULL

AVAILABLE RESOURCE
!=
SUPPLY HUNGER

FOUNDER CAN HUSTLE IT
!=
SELF-PROPULSION

ONE PROFITABLE DEAL
!=
REPEATABLE FLOW
```

Mobilization can only amplify an already evidenced latent connection. It cannot rescue a speculative connection, and it cannot substitute a visible buyer pain for deeper structural-friction discovery.

```text
BUYER COST != ROOT CAUSE
DEMAND PULL != LATENT OUTCOME
STRUCTURAL FRICTION != MISSING EDGE
```

Buyer cost, outsourcing, waiting, search and workaround behavior are powerful mobilization evidence once their causal role is understood; they are not the constitutional starting ontology.

## 3. Bilateral Pull

The preferred route contains observable action on both sides.

### Demand-side pull

Look for actors already:
- hiring;
- searching for suppliers;
- requesting quotes;
- outsourcing;
- paying substitutes;
- delaying work because a capability is missing;
- repeatedly asking for local facts, tests, installation, repair, verification or specialist judgment;
- routing work outside the local region because the desired capability is not callable locally.

### Supply-side hunger

Look for resources/capabilities already:
- seeking work;
- seeking orders;
- seeking customers;
- seeking utilization;
- seeking channels;
- seeking side income;
- seeking distribution;
- seeking application scenarios;
- renting, sharing or discounting idle capacity;
- accepting smaller units of work because full-time/full-project/full-package demand is insufficient.

A candidate with only one active side is not high mobilization.

## 4. Mobilization dimensions

Each dimension is scored `0-3` and every non-zero score requires attributable evidence.

1. `demand_urgency` — is the demand actor already spending, searching, delaying, losing or working around the problem?
2. `supply_hunger` — is the supply actor already actively seeking utilization, income, orders or an application outlet?
3. `resource_abundance` — are there enough replaceable callable resources to route rather than one scarce hero provider?
4. `activation_ease` — can the first useful flow start with low permission, low capital and a bounded task?
5. `value_capture` — after provider payout, QA, failures and acquisition, is there credible economic room for the orchestration layer?
6. `repeatability` — does the underlying actor behavior continually regenerate tasks?
7. `self_propulsion` — once the route is visible, can actors continue entering without founder persuasion on every transaction?
8. `operator_exit` — can the operator eventually leave recurring sales/delivery while task definition, routing, acceptance and settlement remain?

The evidence-backed index is geometric rather than additive. A zero core dimension collapses the index.

## 5. Current-stage priority rule

A route becomes a current-stage high-mobilization candidate only when:

```text
REAL CONNECTION PRESSURE
+
NARROW OBSERVED MISSING EDGE
+
REACHABILITY A/B
+
DEMAND PULL >= 2
+
SUPPLY HUNGER >= 2
+
RESOURCE ABUNDANCE >= 2
+
ACTIVATION EASE >= 2
+
VALUE CAPTURE >= 2
+
REPEATABILITY >= 2
+
SELF-PROPULSION >= 2
+
OPERATOR EXIT >= 2
```

and the recurring model does not require:

- the founder to perform the core delivery;
- the founder to personally sell every transaction.

## 6. Rooter execution map — mandatory for every retained candidate

Every retained scan candidate must be translated into the operator's real-world position:

```text
ACTOR A / demand:
What are they already doing?

ACTOR B / callable resource:
What are they already doing?

PARTIAL FLOW:
How are they already trying to reach each other?

MISSING EDGE:
Exactly what prevents normal flow?

TASK UNIT:
What is the smallest routable unit?

EXECUTOR:
Who actually performs the task?

ACCEPTANCE:
What evidence makes the task accepted?

PAYER:
Who pays and why?

MONEY FLOW:
Payer -> orchestration layer -> capability node

OPERATOR ROLE:
What control-plane work creates value?

OPERATOR NON-ROLE:
What recurring work must never depend on the founder?

EXIT PATH:
How does the founder leave acquisition/delivery while the route keeps flowing?
```

If this map cannot be stated concretely, do not describe the candidate as commercially ready.

> **两边都已经很想动，中间只缺一座桥；根哥搭桥，不背人过河。**

## 8. Anti-story discipline

Do not award mobilization because:
- a market is large;
- many people theoretically have a skill;
- a social problem is important;
- an LLM can imagine a buyer;
- a provider says they are willing to try;
- the founder can personally recruit both sides;
- one actor accepted a cold message;
- the economics work only when founder labor is free.

Prefer observed search, payment, outsourcing, idle-capacity release, workarounds, referrals, job seeking, order seeking and repeated transaction behavior.

## 9. Scan output policy

A scan may still preserve lower-mobilization observations, but the default shortlist shown to the operator should contain at most 3-5 candidates and prioritize:

1. reality validity;
2. reachability;
3. bilateral pull;
4. mobilization;
5. operator fit;
6. cheapest decisive probe.

A truthful but low-mobilization candidate may remain in the ledger without consuming founder attention.
